Healthcare Advisory · the work

  1. Choose what AI may do

    Some choices must stay with people. We draw that line before anyone buys a tool.

    First choose the job. Then choose the AI.

    In a Black Book Research survey of 182 U.S. hospitals, only 22% reported high confidence they could deliver a complete, auditable AI explanation within 30 days. Black Book Research, 2025

  2. Show your work

    Each answer points to the rules and sources behind it. A clinician can check it, question it, and see what changed.

    Clear evidence is better than a confident-sounding answer.

    A 2026 multicenter validation of a widely deployed sepsis model found substantial performance variation across four health systems. Local context matters. So does knowing when the machine should stop talking. Wong et al., JAMA Network Open, 2026

  3. Build for every person

    Doctors, nurses, patients, caregivers, leaders, and families need different things.

    Good AI knows the difference.

    That is the anthropology in the architecture.

  4. Measure what matters

    Use numbers you already track: hospital returns, time saved, clear discharge steps, or triage volume.

    If the work does not improve something you value, do not do it.

Why I do this differently

I started as an archaeologist. I learned to compare the official story with the evidence people leave behind. Then I became an engineer.

I use both skills here. I follow the choice from the patient and clinician to the data and code until the story matches.
