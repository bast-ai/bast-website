EU AI Act

The AI rules apply now.

If your AI talks to people or makes content, Article 50 may apply. You need to show people what the AI is doing.

€15M

Possible fine, or 3% of revenue

An Article 50 violation can bring a fine of up to €15 million or 3% of worldwide yearly revenue, whichever is higher.

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 · Article 99(4)(g) · Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026

SMEs and small mid-cap companies are subject to lower-cap rules.

Some high-risk deadlines moved. Article 50 did not. Most of its transparency rules started on 2 August 2026.

The useful question is simple: who must tell people about the AI, and how will you prove it happened?

  • Up to €35M / 7%

    For AI uses the law bans.

    This is not the Article 50 tier.
  • Up to €15M / 3%

    For Article 50 transparency duties and certain other duties.

    This is the Article 50 ceiling.
  • Up to €7.5M / 1%

    For wrong, missing, or misleading information given to an authority.

  1. If it talks to people

    Tell people they are talking to AI, unless that is already clear.

    Examples: chatbots, assistants, voice agents, and intake tools.

  2. If it makes content

    Add a machine-readable mark to synthetic audio, images, video, or text.

    Some technical and editing exceptions apply.

  3. If it reads people or publishes

    Tell people about emotion or biometric tools. Label covered deepfakes and public-interest text.

    The Act has exceptions. Other privacy laws still apply.

Regulatory Briefing · the work

What the work looks like

A rule only works when a person owns it.

Legal can read the law. Technology can build the control. Product and operations must use it. I help those groups agree on one clear plan.

  1. Find the AI in use

    List the systems that talk to people, make content, read emotion or biometrics, or publish covered material.

    Look at what is running now—not last year’s strategy.

  2. Name one owner

    Each duty needs a person. The same AI can give different organizations different jobs under the law.

    Who adds the notice? Who checks the mark? Who approves an exception?

  3. Keep proof

    For each system, record the owner, rule, method, version, and evidence that the control worked.

    A policy says what you planned. Evidence shows what happened.
