Change

Help your best people do more.

Your people need to know what the AI is for, what stays human, and who owns the result.

The hard part is agreement

A tool does not make a team ready. Leaders must make one clear plan, explain it, and keep it working when the tool or people change.

People do not fight clear change. They fight vague goals, hidden risk, and work done to them instead of with them.

  1. Leaders need plain words

    People should hear the same reason for the change from every leader.

    Leaders do not need to code. They need to ask good questions and explain the choice.

    70% of the difference in team engagement can be traced to the manager.Gallup

    Costs you: trust and speed.

  2. Use your best people

    AI should give people more time for the work they do best.

    Start with that goal—not headcount.

    18 points separate employees whose manager supports AI use from those whose manager does not.Gallup, 43,262 U.S. employees, 2026

    Costs you: your best people checking out.

  3. Agree on the job

    The board, finance, legal, technology, and employees often want different things.

    Put those goals in one plan.

    15 points is the engagement advantage when people say there is a clear plan for using AI at work.Gallup, 43,262 U.S. employees, 2026

    Costs you: slow choices and stalled work.

Change & Adoption · the work

  • Weeks 1–3

    See the real work. Map the choices, handoffs, workarounds, and places where the plan breaks.

    You get: one clear map of what is happening now.
  • Weeks 4–10

    Make one plan. Put leaders, builders, risk owners, and the people doing the work around the same choices.

    You get: clear owners, limits, and a way to handle disagreement.
  • Weeks 11–16

    Make it stick. Test what happens when the AI is wrong, a person disagrees, a leader leaves, or the market changes.

    You get: a plan your people can keep using without me.

What you own at the end

  • A shared goalEveryone can say what AI is for—and what it is not for.
  • Leaders who can explain itEnough understanding to make the choice and answer honest questions.
  • A plan that lastsIt stays useful when the tool, market, or people change.

Why I do this differently

I started as an archaeologist. I learned to look at what people really do, not just what the plan says.

As an engineer, I learned to move hard work forward without managing every person involved. That takes clear choices, evidence, and trust.

I advise three organizations at a time so I can stay close to the people doing the work.
