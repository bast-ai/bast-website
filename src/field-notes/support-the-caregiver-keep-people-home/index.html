Field notes

Support the caregiver, keep people home.

November 13, 2026

Columbia researchers sent daily support messages to 966 African American and Hispanic family caregivers of people with dementia for 12 months. 82.3% stayed in for 9 months or more, and the number of isolated caregivers fell 46.2% (Yoon et al., npj Mental Health Research). Both study groups got support, so the trial had no group without it.

Idaho's Family Caregiver Navigator gives caregivers a no-cost phone service with a needs check, matched resources and follow-up. Among 289 caregivers in the program, burden scores on the Zarit-4 and depression scores improved, and 58% said they were less likely to move their person into a facility (Toevs et al., International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health). This was a program review with no comparison group.

In a Kansas pilot, 121 Medicaid members who qualified for nursing-home care lived at home with in-home services, home sensors and daily vital-sign checks. Their total nursing-facility days fell 54% compared with the time before the pilot (Zemplee and Windsor Place At-Home Care). The sensor company published these results, and the pilot had no comparison group.

Our take: steady support lifts caregivers. When caregivers carry less, families keep care at home. Support the caregiver, and you support the patient.
