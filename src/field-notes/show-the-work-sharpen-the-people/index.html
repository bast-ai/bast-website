Field notes

Show the work, sharpen the people.

November 13, 2026

At Vanderbilt, 2,385 patients with a positive ANA blood test were split at random into two groups. In one group, a clear, readable risk model flagged high-risk patients and offered them a fast visit with a rheumatologist. Among patients who were diagnosed with autoimmune disease, the median time to diagnosis fell from 138 days to 43 (Barnado et al., NEJM AI). The trial ran at one health system.

In a randomized test, 50 radiologists and 75 other doctors and trainees read head CT scans. Correct AI advice that marked its findings on the scan raised their accuracy compared with no advice (odds ratio 1.84) and raised their confidence. Readers rated the marked-up advice more useful than plain AI advice, and novices valued it most (Gaube et al., The Lancet Digital Health). The test used past scans in a controlled setting.

Our take: AI should show its work. When people can see the why, they act faster and with more confidence.
